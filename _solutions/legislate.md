Legislate.4 is for parliamentary counsel and law revision offices. A defect in a law is found sooner or later: by a drafter, a court, or someone who falls through a gap. Many survive careful reading because they appear only when the rules run, when a date rolls over, a second person arrives, or two provisions meet. Legislate.4 looks for them while fixing one still costs a sentence.

## What it does

- **Probes thirteen categories of defect**: definition, reference, logic, time, uncertainty, power, enforcement, amendment, interaction, extrinsic material, workability, form and style, each by a written procedure, over every Part encoded.
- **Ties every finding to evidence**: a test, a scenario, a simulation run or a quotation, traceable to a provision. A finding with nothing behind it is labelled opinion.
- **Records forks, not just faults**: where the text supports two readings, both are recorded, with the reading taken and why.
- **Leads with plain headlines**: up to ten findings, each told as what happens to whom, with drafting notes and the technical analysis one step away.
- **Hands over a console**: the drafter replays each finding day by day, runs simulations of months or years of lives under the law, and records a verdict on each finding to export as their review.

## How it works

The law is written in L4, in its own terms, with tests taken from the text. Encoding is a test in itself: every term needs a meaning, every deadline a starting day, every case an outcome. The work runs in fourteen steps. Agents encode, probe, demonstrate and challenge each candidate against the Interpretation Act, regulations and explanatory material. People sign four gates: the jurisdiction's reference set, its completeness, the fidelity of the encoding to the law, and each release.

The result is a written report and the console, built from one register. The console runs in a browser with no installation and no model behind it: scenarios and simulations play from the encoding itself. It separates what the law could produce from what was simply assumed, so a defect reached that way is shown to arise from the law. The encoding that has been checked can later serve as a decision service.

## Where it comes from

Legislate.4 grew out of quality assurance work for a legislative drafting office: a full run over two Parts of an animal-registration Act and its regulations, giving 55 findings backed by over a hundred machine-checked tests and forty console scenarios, and a benchmark on a short federal Bill. The pipeline is in its first version.
