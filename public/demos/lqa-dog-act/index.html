The scheme definition — this is the entire statute-specific input

Below is the whole of what makes this the Act rather than any other, including what the console asks you for when you create a new actor. It is data: no functions, no statute-specific code. engine.js reads it and has never heard of a dog or a barring order.


    

  


  

    
What this is. A working demonstration that a legislative simulation console can be a product rather than a bespoke build. Three statutes run on one engine here; a fourth would be a fourth scheme and no change to the engine.

    
Three modes. In a Scenario you write the events yourself. In God mode you may set any fact, which is how you set up a case to show someone; in Nature mode only what could really happen: actors are born at nought, age is worked out from a date of birth against a real calendar, and every other fact has to be conferred by somebody's act. In a Simulation the app writes the events itself, at rates you set, in Nature mode's terms. What each mode asks for is declared by the scheme, not by the console.

    
Nature constrains, law does not. Nature mode refuses the impossible and permits the unlawful, because a console that would not let you break the law could never show you an offence. A third case sits between them: an act by somebody with no power to do it is allowed, and does nothing at all. All three are here — a dog cannot be whelped aged ten, a shopkeeper may purport to declare a dog dangerous and achieve nothing, and a restricted breed pup may be handed over in a transfer that is both effective and a crime.

    
What it is not. No scheme is a complete model of its Act. Each covers the provisions named above it, which is enough to show the method and the findings, and not enough to advise anyone. Neither the Retail Barring Orders Bill nor the Rent Cap Bill is law. Nothing here is legal advice, and no person, dog, shop or incident in it is real.

    
Honest about the engine. The expression language is deliberately small — booleans, comparisons, arithmetic, relationship lookups, named definitions and a day counter. Real drafting offices would need more, and the point of keeping it declarative is that more can be added without rewriting a single scheme.
