From 12 to 14 July 2023, SMU CCLAW hosted the first Computational Law Conference in Singapore. The theme — Computational Law + Symbolic AI + Industry Adoption — brought together computer scientists, legal scholars, and practitioners in a way that few conferences in this space have managed. We presented on L4's technical architecture to an audience that included some of the sharpest minds in the field.

The most useful sessions were about the hard problems: defeasibility, vagueness, and the gap between research-grade tools and production-ready systems. Most organisations attending were not yet in a position to encode their regulatory obligations in L4 — not because the technology is unready, but because the workflows, tooling, and professional expertise are still being developed. That is the commercialisation challenge Legalese is working on.

The timing — with LLMs now part of every conversation about legal AI — made one thing clearer than ever. The field needs both: probabilistic models for language tasks, symbolic systems for rule application. CLAWCON gave that thesis a serious audience. We will be back.
