Legalese has joined the Insurance Initiative at Stanford's CodeX Centre for Legal Informatics. The initiative focuses on applying computational law approaches to insurance — a sector built on rules, where the gap between the precision required for correct outcomes and the ambiguity inherent in natural language policy documents is particularly costly.

Every coverage determination is structurally a rule application problem: does this event, in these circumstances, trigger this coverage? That question should have a deterministic answer. In practice, the rules encoded in policy documents are ambiguous enough that disputes are common and outcomes are inconsistent. L4 offers a path to encoding insurance policies as precise, executable specifications — where every coverage question can be computed and every decision can be audited.

Our contribution to the initiative draws on L4's defeasible deontic logic foundations and the controlled natural language that bridges legal text and formal specification. Insurance policies are complex but more structured than most legislation, making them an ideal domain for demonstrating what computable contracts can do in practice.
