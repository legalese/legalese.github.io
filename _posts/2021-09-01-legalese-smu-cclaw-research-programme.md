Since 2020, Legalese has been the lead industry partner for the Research Programme in Computational Law at Singapore Management University's Centre for Computational Law (SMU CCLAW). L4 is open source — our role is to drive adoption and real-world application, working alongside the research team to build the tools and workflows that make L4 useful in practice.

The central challenge is expressing legal rules with enough precision that a computer can execute them, while remaining readable enough that a lawyer can validate the result. Most legal AI sidesteps this by using probabilistic models that learn patterns from past decisions. These are useful for some tasks, but they cannot do the thing that matters most in high-stakes contexts: tell you with certainty whether a situation is compliant or not.

L4 is our answer. It is a domain-specific programming language grounded in deontic modal logic — the formal system underlying legal reasoning. The SMU CCLAW programme brings together computer scientists, legal scholars, and linguists to work on the hard problems: defeasibility, vagueness, temporal obligations. We will write more about specific advances as the programme develops.
